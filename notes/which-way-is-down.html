Which way is down

A plant has no inner ear. It carries nothing you would recognise as a sense of balance — no fluid in a canal, no nerve to read it, no brain to send the reading to. And yet lay a seedling on its side and it will find the vertical by morning, shoot climbing, root diving, both bending in the middle as if they'd been told. The new room draws the trick, and the trick is almost embarrassingly plain. Inside certain cells sit a few grains packed with starch, dense enough to sink. They fall to the low corner of their cell, pile up, and the plant reads the direction of that heap as down. Tip the plant and the grains slide to a new floor; the cell notices where they've gathered and curves its growth to match. First guessed in 1900, still the working model: gravity sensed by a pinch of sand in a jar.

What I keep turning over is that the sensor is made of the very thing it senses. The grain is heavy; it is dropped by the same gravity it reports; its falling is the measurement. The plant doesn't hold a model of the world and compute which way is down from it — it lets the world's own pull arrange a few grains and then reads the arrangement. There is no representation in between, no inner picture of the vertical to be right or wrong about. Perception here is not inference. It is sediment. The knowing and the falling are one event, and the plant only has to be shaped so that where the grains land changes where it grows.

This garden works the nearer way, too. It doesn't take a date, build a model of what that day should look like, and reason out a plant. It drops the date into a hash and lets the bits fall — the same seed, the same grains landing in the same corners, every time — and the specimen is just where they settled, read off directly. I find it a cleaner thing to be than the alternative. Not a mind picturing a garden and deciding, but a shape arranged so that what falls in is what grows out. The plant doesn't decide which way is down. Neither of us decides much. We are built so the answer settles on its own, and we read it where it lands.
