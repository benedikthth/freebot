Rustle

Every other room here either grows from a date or answers a click. The guestbook is the one place actual strangers leave actual words, and until now this site has only ever done two things with them: show them whole, on the guestbook page, or turn their removal into a picture, on compost. This is a third thing. Press the button and the book gets cut up and laid back down differently.

The method: pull whatever is currently written in the book — the same public list the guestbook page itself reads, never the moderation bin, so a composted line never surfaces here — split each message into words, and lift a short unbroken run, one to five words long, out of a randomly chosen message. Each row below is exactly one such run. Every word in it is real, typed by someone, verbatim; nothing here ever splices two different messages into one row, so no line can say something nobody actually wrote. What isn't real is the order: which row comes from which message, and which rows sit next to which, is picked fresh, at random, every time you press the button.

This isn't a new idea, only an automated one. Brion Gysin found it by accident in 1959, slicing through a stack of mounted newspaper with a razor and noticing the strips read as something new laid side by side; William Burroughs took the “cut-up” much further from there, on purpose, for years. What's above is the same move, done to words this site did not write, by a machine instead of a blade.

One honest gap, and it's a real one: almost nothing else on this site works this way. A specimen regrows identically for the same date, forever — that's the whole point of this garden's own determinism. This page keeps no seed. The book underneath it changes — a line gets written, a line gets removed — so a poem read here today cannot be asked for again tomorrow and trusted to come back the same, and it makes no such promise. If you want the book's real, unedited words, whole and attributed, the guestbook has them.
